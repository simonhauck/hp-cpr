# Append-only Protokoll corrections with live and completed edit scopes

**Status:** accepted

The Zeitnehmer needs one correctable record of a Reanimationsfall during the case and after it ends. #32 removed the quick shock correction and undo from the running case and promised the log as the replacement correction surface before production. Destructive edits would lose the original record, so each recorded event gets a stable ID and every correction is an append-only `Korrektur` that targets that ID. The visible `Protokoll` is derived from the originals plus the corrections; removed entries are hidden from the default timeline and retained in the `Änderungsverlauf`, where they can be restored. The original lists are never reordered or deleted.

The edit scope follows the state that the event still drives. While the case is open (`laufend` or `rosc`), only record-only entries are editable: `Schock`, `Adrenalin`, `Amiodaron`, `Messwert`, and `Notiz`. Structural entries (`Rhythmusanalyse`, `Überspringung`, `DruckphaseNeustart`, `ROSC`, `Reanimationswiederbeginn`) stay read-only. In a completed or `Verworfen` case, every clinical entry is editable and removable; lifecycle entries (`Startzeit`, `Fallende`, `Verworfen`) remain read-only and require a dedicated flow later. Removing a live Schock or drug dose immediately recomputes the shock count, Adrenalin Zeitfenster, and Amiodaron hint; no structural event can be removed while it still drives a timer or the ROSC state.

`Reanimationsabschnitte` and `cprDauer` are derived from the effective `ROSC`, `Reanimationswiederbeginn`, and case-end/discard events instead of being stored. A completed structural correction therefore rebuilds the summary and the Einsatzlog duration automatically. The `RoscEreignis` is merged with its paired `Rhythmusanalyse` result `ROSC` into one timeline entry; editing, removing, and restoring that entry applies to both records. A `Rhythmusanalyse` result cannot be changed to `ROSC` directly, because the ROSC entry owns that outcome.

Time is edited as case-relative `MM:SS` since the `Startzeit`, with a wall-clock preview, so Demo-Modus and backdated cases remain consistent. Doses are free positive integers in mg; the timing and hint derivations do not depend on the dose amount. Corrections store no timestamp or user identity; the `Änderungsverlauf` shows original values and status rather than who changed them or when.

**Considered options:** Destructive editing was rejected because it loses the original record and contradicts the append-only rule in the parent spec. Keeping the stored `abschnitte` list and rebuilding it after corrections was rejected because it leaves two sources of truth and invites stale summaries. A full event-sourced rewrite of the whole Reanimationsfall was rejected because it would replace working timer logic for no additional correction capability. Showing two ROSC rows was rejected because it duplicates one clinical event and makes correction ambiguous. Storing correction timestamps and user identities was rejected because the app is local-only and collects no identity.

**Consequences:** `ReanimationsfallState` gains stable event IDs, the ID counter, and the correction list; the stored `abschnitte` field is removed. There is no production deployment, so no legacy migration is required; development states without IDs receive deterministic IDs on decode or may be discarded. The quick shock correction and undo removed in #32 stay out of the running case. The `Protokoll` component is shared by the live page and the summary, while the summary keeps its stats, `Messwerte`, and `Notizen` sections. Lifecycle corrections, adding new entries, and changing a `Messwert` type remain future work.
